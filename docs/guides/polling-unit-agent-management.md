# How Polling Unit Agent Management Works on Election Day

Polling Unit Agents are a critical part of election-day operations because they connect what is happening at individual Polling Units with the campaign's wider monitoring structure.

The challenge is not simply finding volunteers. A campaign needs to know **who is assigned, whether the person is approved, where they are deployed, and whether the Polling Unit is reporting as expected**.

## Start before election day

Agent management should begin well before voting starts. A practical workflow usually covers:

1. identifying potential agents;
2. collecting the information required by the campaign;
3. reviewing suitability and identity information;
4. approving or rejecting requests;
5. assigning the approved person to the correct Polling Unit;
6. confirming deployment and communication arrangements.

If these decisions are postponed until election morning, campaign leadership has little time to correct missing coverage.

## Use clear entry paths

Campaigns often recruit agents in different ways. Campaign Manager's verified workflow supports:

- **Self Request** — a member indicates interest;
- **Leader Nomination** — a campaign leader nominates someone;
- **Admin Appointment** — an authorised administrator appoints an agent;
- **Super Admin Assignment** — top-level administration assigns an agent directly.

Different campaigns may use these routes differently, but separating them helps leadership understand how each agent entered the process.

See the product-specific [Polling Unit Agent Workflow](../polling-unit-agent-workflow.md).

## Track status instead of relying on memory

An agent record should have a clear status. Campaign Manager's verified workflow includes:

- Pending;
- Approved;
- Rejected;
- Suspended;
- Revoked.

This makes it possible to distinguish a person who expressed interest from someone who is actually authorised to represent the campaign at a Polling Unit.

## Match agents to geography

The campaign should know which Polling Unit each approved agent covers. That connection makes coverage measurable.

For example, leadership should be able to identify:

- Polling Units with approved agents;
- Polling Units without agents;
- duplicate or conflicting assignments;
- agents assigned outside their expected geography where an authorised override is used.

Campaign Manager includes a campaign deployment override workflow for authorised assignments when operational deployment differs from a person's registered Polling Unit.

## Protect identity information

Agent onboarding may involve sensitive identity information. Campaign teams should minimise access and avoid circulating these records in open chat groups or unsecured spreadsheets.

Do not publish identity documents, voter evidence, phone numbers, addresses or personal records in public repositories or screenshots.

## Define election-day reporting expectations

Every agent should know what the campaign expects and when. This may include operational check-ins, incident reporting and result-submission procedures according to the campaign's legal and operational plan.

The important principle is consistency: every Polling Unit should follow the same reporting workflow wherever possible.

## Connect agent coverage to the Situation Room

Election-day operations become much easier to monitor when agent assignments and Polling Unit reporting can be viewed together.

Leadership can then ask:

- Which Polling Units are active?
- Which are silent?
- Which have submitted results?
- Which submissions are verified or disputed?
- Where have incidents been reported?

Campaign Manager's [Election Situation Room](../election-situation-room.md) is designed around these coverage and exception signals.

## Plan escalation paths

Agents should not have to guess whom to contact when something goes wrong. Before election day, define escalation routes for:

- missing materials or operational problems;
- safety incidents;
- disputed reporting;
- evidence submission;
- communication failure;
- replacement or reassignment of an agent.

A structured escalation process helps prevent every problem from being sent directly to national leadership.

## The operational goal

Good Polling Unit Agent management gives campaign leadership confidence that each important Polling Unit has a known, approved person and that gaps can be identified quickly.
