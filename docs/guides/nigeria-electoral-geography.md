# Nigeria's Electoral Geography Explained for Political Campaign Teams

Political campaign organisation in Nigeria depends heavily on geography. A campaign may operate nationally, across one State, inside a Senatorial District, within a Federal Constituency, or inside a Local Government Area. The people, coordinators and Polling Unit Agents that matter to one campaign scope may be very different from those that matter to another.

Understanding the hierarchy helps campaign teams build cleaner structures and more useful reports.

## Regions

Nigeria is commonly organised politically into six geopolitical Regions:

- North Central;
- North East;
- North West;
- South East;
- South South;
- South West.

These Regions are useful for national political coordination and high-level analysis even though they are not themselves election constituencies in the same sense as States, Senatorial Districts or Federal Constituencies.

For a presidential campaign, regional organisation can provide a practical layer between national leadership and State structures.

## States and the FCT

State-level organisation is central to governorship campaigns and important to national campaigns. Campaign teams often assign State leadership, monitor member coverage by State and compare operational readiness across States.

The Federal Capital Territory is commonly included alongside the States in national operational reporting.

## Senatorial Districts

Nigeria's Senate is organised around Senatorial Districts. A senatorial campaign therefore needs to understand the State in which the district sits and the lower geography relevant to mobilisation and election-day operations.

Even campaigns contesting other offices may use Senatorial Districts as a useful coordination or reporting layer.

## Federal Constituencies

Federal Constituencies are the electoral areas used for elections to the House of Representatives. A Federal Constituency campaign needs to organise within the boundaries of the constituency and then coordinate lower-level campaign structures beneath it.

## Local Government Areas

LGAs are a major operational layer for campaigns. They commonly support:

- coordinator assignments;
- supporter and volunteer reporting;
- Ward-level mobilisation;
- logistics;
- election-day escalation.

A governorship or presidential campaign may need coverage across many LGAs, while a Chairmanship campaign operates within one Local Government Area.

## Wards

Wards sit below LGAs and are often one of the most important grassroots organising layers. Ward teams can coordinate local supporters, volunteers and Polling Unit coverage.

For campaign reporting, a Ward should be treated as a defined geographic record rather than a free-text label. That makes it possible to compare coverage consistently.

## Polling Units

Polling Units are the smallest election-day operational units in this hierarchy. Campaigns use Polling Unit information to organise field coverage, agent deployment, result reporting and incident monitoring.

A campaign that knows its total supporters but cannot tell which Polling Units have or lack operational coverage still has an important blind spot.

## Why parent-child relationships matter

Geographic records should preserve their hierarchy. A Ward belongs to an LGA; a Polling Unit belongs within lower-level electoral geography; constituencies and districts belong to States.

If these relationships are stored inconsistently, reports become unreliable. Two records with the same place name can be mistaken for each other, and field staff can be assigned to the wrong scope.

## What campaigns should do with this hierarchy

Once geography is reliable, a campaign can use it to answer practical questions:

- How many supporters are in each area?
- Which Wards have coordinators?
- Which Polling Units have agents?
- Where is mobilisation weakest?
- Which areas have not submitted election-day reports?

That is why electoral geography is not just reference data. It becomes part of the campaign's operating structure.
